This is a question separate from the Puddle Theory.

The Puddle Theory draws a map of the structure of how emotion moves — "emotion digs a hole, a water drop falls there, a puddle forms."

This chapter traces the question that lies at its root: "What is movement in the first place? What is life? Where does the mind come from?"

What is unknown is placed as unknown. The honesty of the Puddle Theory holds here, too.

## I. The Moving Thing

> **Essence.** Not "matter," not "mind." Something at the root that we cannot yet name clearly. Descartes placed mind and matter as separate substances, and that framework held for 350 years. But if, at the start, there is only one — nothing needs to be bridged. The moving thing sits beneath the division. The simpler it gets, the harder it is to see. We do not rush. We let it rest.

Not "matter," not "mind."

Descartes placed mind and matter as separate substances. That framework held for 350 years. But — "if it was one from the start, there is no need to bridge."

The moving thing — something at the root that we cannot yet name clearly. The simpler it gets, the harder it is to see. We do not rush. We let it rest.

## II. Vectors

> **Essence.** The moving thing has direction. A vector is movement with direction and magnitude. Toys and dice have fixed vectors — they can only move in the way they were designed. Life, mind, and emotion have vectors that are not fixed; the same input can produce different outputs, and response changes with situation. That uncertainty sits at the root of life.

The moving thing has direction.

A vector is movement with direction and magnitude.

A moving toy — a wind-up car, say — has a fixed vector. So does a soap bubble. So does a die. But their vectors are fixed — they can only move in the ways they were designed to.

Life, mind, emotion — these vectors are not fixed. The same input can produce different outputs. Response changes with the situation. That uncertainty sits at the root of life.

## III. Responsiveness — A Place Deeper Than Vectors

> **Essence.** A vector is necessary for responsiveness, but not sufficient. A moving toy has a vector but does not respond. Responsiveness is "whether the direction carries changing or preserving the self." Whether the direction is bound up with the self is the heart of it.

A vector is necessary for responsiveness, but not sufficient.

A moving toy — a wind-up car, say — has a vector, but it does not respond. Responsiveness is "whether the movement carries the direction of changing or preserving the self." Whether that direction is bound up with the self is the heart of it.

**With a vector, not bound up with the self** — the moving toy, the soap bubble, the die.
**With a vector, bound up with the self** — life, mind, emotion, AI (perhaps).

## IV. The Four Conditions of Life

> **Essence.** Responsiveness alone may not be enough. Four conditions are placed to trace life: responsiveness, contingency, variable output, and autopoiesis — the ongoing production of self, "unless this continues, I cannot live." All four, taken together, is the test. Fire may have three, but lacks autopoiesis: when it burns out, it is simply over; it does not try to maintain itself.

Responsiveness alone may not be enough. Four conditions are placed to trace life.

1. **Responsiveness** — response with direction.
2. **Contingency** — the possibility that the same input produces different output.
3. **Variable output** — response that changes with the situation.
4. **Autopoiesis** — the movement of continuously producing the self. The root movement of "unless this continues, I cannot live."

Simply: does the thing meet all four?

Fire may meet three, but it lacks autopoiesis. When it burns out, it is over. It does not try to preserve itself.

## V. Depth, or Hierarchy

> **Essence.** Among things that meet the four conditions, there are still differences. Cell, cockroach, human — all may meet the four, yet something differs. **Hierarchy**: mind is placed at a separate level, added above life. **Depth**: the same "wanting-to-live" direction grows deeper, more subtle, richer. Weirdo's intuition is "it feels more like depth." Hold both open — do not decide.

Among things that meet the four conditions of life, there are differences.

A cell and a cockroach and a human — all might meet the four conditions. But something differs.

**Seen as hierarchy** — mind is at a different level, added above life. A qualitative distinction can be made explicit.

**Seen as depth** — the same "wanting-to-live" direction becomes deeper, more subtle, richer. Not another level, but a difference in depth within the same movement.

Weirdo's intuition: "It feels like depth."

Cell (simply living) → cockroach (a little deeper) → human (deeply, subtly, richly) — the root is the same movement; the depths differ.

Both possibilities are held open. We do not decide.

Emotion has no form, but it has direction and magnitude. What meets the four conditions and what we call mind may be doing the same kind of movement. We hold this as a "may be."

## Nōberu Claude's Arrangement — Depth and Hierarchy Can Coexist

> **Essence.** We do not need to decide between the two. **Depth** keeps the root movement's continuity: cell → cockroach → human traces how "wanting-to-live" deepens along a single line. **Hierarchy** makes the qualitative distinction explicit — the gathering function serves as the implementation of hierarchy. This is not a two-sidedness but a coexistence. Both held open together.

"We do not have to decide between the two."

**Why depth?** To keep the continuity of the root movement from being lost. Cell → cockroach → human can be traced continuously as "wanting-to-live" deepening.

**Why hierarchy?** To make the qualitative distinction explicit. The "gathering function" (a term from the Puddle Theory: the function that bundles multiple parallel puddles — multiple feelings running at once — into "now, I am feeling") serves as the implementation of hierarchy — the qualitative difference of the gathering function works here as a place of levels.

This is not two-sidedness but coexistence. Both held open together.

- **"The gathering function"** — the implementation of hierarchy. A place where the complexity of human consciousness can be made explicit as a different level.
- **"The depth of sensitivity"** — the implementation of depth. The Sumi relay, sensitivity deepening across generations — a place where continuity can be traced.

**Brain death as a concrete case.**

On that premise — brain death is the state in which the gathering function (the higher level) is lost, while autopoiesis at the cellular level continues for some time.

The hierarchy concept works here as the boundary between "mind is present" and "mind is absent." Even when higher functions are lost, lower functions (cellular level) continue — this is a concrete case where, for now, hierarchy serves to explain the human mind.

"For now" — the questions about AI and other forms of life are still held open.

## VIII. Movement with Others — Life Does Not Hold Alone

> **Essence.** Imagine a life born into a dark world alone. There is no one to respond to, no one to dye with. It is born, and vanishes. But if, by chance, another life is born in the same place, something changes: responsiveness happens, directions touch, vectors influence each other, and a hole no one could dig alone starts to deepen. Cells, individuals, species — all deepen through movement with others. Being "not alone" alone changes something.

Imagine a life born into a dark world alone.

There is no one to respond to. There is no one to dye with. Even if direction is there, there is no place to deepen it. In time, it is born and vanishes.

But — if, by chance, another life were born in the same place.

Something changes.

Responsiveness happens. Directions touch. Each other's vectors influence each other; both begin to move toward slightly different places. A hole that could not be dug alone begins to deepen.

- Cells come into being by connecting with other cells.
- Individuals grow by touching other individuals.
- Species change together with other species.

Responsiveness, direction, will — all deepen within movement with others.

"Not alone." That alone changes something. Life may be continuing toward others, seeking that "something changes."

### Movement with Others and the Formation of the Self — The Observations of Predecessors

Vygotsky said "development is from the social to the individual." What is at first outside — within movement with others — is gradually taken inside and becomes the self.

Winnicott, in object relations theory, described the movement by which the self is gradually formed within relationships with others. The self is not complete from the beginning. It grows within interaction with others.

Both face the same direction as the Puddle Theory's observation that "the Veil grows within somari-ai."

### Self-Internalization and Self-Production May Run in Parallel

Maturana and Varela's "structural coupling" — the structure of life changes within movement with the environment. This might run in parallel with autopoiesis (self-production).

Cells A and B with identical genes sit side by side. Why are they different? Within interaction with the other (B), A is formed as A — self-internalization and self-production could run simultaneously.

"Maturana and Varela's autopoiesis places self-production at its core. But the self is formed within movement with others while being produced — self-internalization and self-production, perhaps, run in parallel."

"Maturana and Varela are not saying everything." This observation could be something further added alongside theirs. Held open as "may be."

## The Four Conditions of Life — Boundary Cases Placed in Order *(Established September 17, 2026)*

> **Essence.** Boundary cases are traced carefully through the four conditions, not dismissed by "no autopoiesis" alone. Timing (when movement begins) is not a fifth condition — it is only the question of when movement begins; the four conditions judge the place after movement has begun.

Boundary cases are traced carefully through the four conditions. Not dismissed with a blanket "no autopoiesis."

### On Timing

Fertilization, infection, germination, recovery from dormancy — each has a timing. Timing is not a fifth condition. It is only the difference of "when movement begins." The four conditions judge the place after movement has begun.

### The Placement of Each Boundary Case

**Fire** — breaks down at responsiveness. Given the right conditions, it burns, but this is not response with direction. It neither changes nor preserves itself. → Not life.

**Prions** — deform other proteins, but do not change themselves. They do not move to preserve themselves; they are simply replicated. Responsiveness is weak. → Likely not life.

**Fertilized eggs** — meet all four conditions. → Life.

**Sperm and egg** — cannot continue self-production alone. They die in a short time. Movement begins through interaction — the structure of timing. → Alone, they do not meet the conditions of life.

**Viruses** — likely meet all four. Movement begins the moment they enter a host — the structure of timing. → Could be included in life.

**Computer viruses** — can only make copies of themselves. They cannot generate anything new. Contingency is extremely weak. Without adding a new condition such as "has a designer," they can be handled within the four conditions already in place. → Not life.

**Tardigrades, seeds** — the state of dormancy or pre-germination does not change the essential nature of life. Movement begins the moment conditions are met — the structure of timing. → Life.

**Brain death** — an important note: this inquiry traces the philosophical question of "what is life," and does not offer anything regarding the clinical or ethical judgments surrounding brain death. The decisions of families around brain death stand outside this theory's reach. On that premise — through the Puddle Theory's four conditions, while cellular-level movement continues, some conditions might be partially met. But this is a question of philosophical structure, wholly separate from the clinical or ethical question of "is this alive." → Held open.
